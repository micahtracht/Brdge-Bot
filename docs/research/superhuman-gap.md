# Superhuman Bridge AI: What It Would Actually Require

> Research report, 2026-08-14.

## 1. History of human vs. computer bridge matches

**Zia Mahmood's bet (1990s).** In *Bridge, My Way* (1992), Zia Mahmood offered a £1M bet that no computer would beat a four-person team of his choosing; he withdrew the wager in 1996 as programs improved ([Wikipedia: Computer bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_bridge)). The bet was never actually contested — it was withdrawn, not lost.

**GIB and the 1998 Par Contest (Lille).** Matthew Ginsberg's GIB won the 1998 World Computer Bridge Championship and was invited into the human Par Contest at the 1998 world championships — a *declarer-play problem-solving* contest, not a real match. GIB placed 12th (score 11,210) against 34 top humans including Zia ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_bridge); [GIB IJCAI-99 paper](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/99-1/Papers/084.pdf)). Criticism: par contests test technical card-play analysis on composed deals, with no bidding, no partner, no inference from real auctions.

**Zia vs. programs, 1999.** In an individual round-robin match format, Zia beat the field of programs including GIB ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_bridge)) — i.e., the year after GIB's par-contest showing, a top human still won a more game-like format.

**Jack vs. Dutch experts (2005–06).** The strongest "real bridge" test to date: five-time computer champion Jack played a series against seven top Dutch pairs (including European champions). Jack beat three of seven pairs and lost overall by a small margin, 359–385 IMPs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_bridge)). This was full bridge (bidding + play), and the bots were competitive with, but below, top-pair level.

**World Computer-Bridge Championship (1997–2019).** Annual bot-vs-bot event (Al Levy, ACBL/WBF): Jack won ~10 titles, WBridge5 8 titles from 11 finals; the last official edition (23rd, San Francisco 2019) was won by Micro Bridge; the event paused for Covid and never officially resumed — only "unofficial" championships since 2022 ([bridgebotchampionship.com](https://bridgebotchampionship.com/home/world-computer-bridge-championship/), [computerbridge.se](https://www.computerbridge.se/world-computer-bridge-championship/)). Consequence: there is currently no official bot-vs-bot benchmark event, and **no bot has ever beaten a top human partnership/team at full bridge**. The Wikipedia summary is blunt: unlike chess, computer bridge "has not reached world-class dominance."

## 2. NooK / NukkAI Challenge (March 24–25, 2022, Paris)

**Exact format.** Eight world-champion humans (including Nevena Senior and Cédric Lorenzini) each played 10 sets of 10 deals; NooK played all 80 sets (800 deals). **No bidding**: every deal was a 3NT contract reached via a canned auction (essentially 1NT–3NT sequences), with human/AI always declarer and the defenders always the *same bot pair, WBridge5* (not NukkAI's). Human and AI declared identical deals against identical defenders; a set was won by the higher total over 10 deals. NooK won 67 of 80 sets (83%) ([challenge.nukk.ai](https://challenge.nukk.ai/), [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/radio/asithappens/as-it-happens-the-wednesday-edition-1.6402751/an-artificial-intelligence-just-beat-8-world-champions-at-bridge-1.6402861), [Singularity Hub](https://singularityhub.com/2022/04/03/a-hybrid-ai-just-beat-eight-world-champions-at-bridge-and-explained-how-it-did-it/)).

**Expert criticism.**

- **Kit Woolsey** (analysis serialized on Bridgewinners, summarized at [taogaming.wordpress.com](https://taogaming.wordpress.com/2022/05/15/kit-woolseys-analysis-of-nooks-match/); BW articles: [Anna vs. Nook](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/anna-vs-nook/), [Mikael vs. Nook](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/mikael-vs-nook/), [Conclusions](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/conclusions/)): the score is meaningless for comparing NooK to human experts because the two sides were "playing two different games — the experts were playing 'bridge' while Nook was playing 'exploit WB5'." NooK could learn/exploit WBridge5's deterministic defensive tendencies; humans couldn't. Woolsey's verdict: NooK "is not a world-class declarer" and "makes too many glaring errors."
- **Scope**: declarer play with a fixed 3NT contract and no auction information is at best one-third of the game — no bidding, no defense, no partnership cooperation, and even the declarer-play test omitted trump contracts and auction-based inference ([LessWrong: "So has AI conquered Bridge?"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yHxmJch8dJoH6dwwz/so-has-ai-conquered-bridge)).
- **Björn Hjalmarsson (Bridgewinners)** ran NooK's deals through WBridge5 5.12 itself: NooK 729 vs. WBridge5 701 on ~100 filtered boards — "at par with WBridge5 but not much more," with unknowns about which WBridge5 version/settings NukkAI used and NooK's thinking time ([The NukkAI Challenge — WBridge5 vs Nook](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/the-nukkai-challenge-wbridge5-vs-nook-nukkai-ai/), [computerbridge.se](https://www.computerbridge.se/copy-of-the-nukkai-challenge/)). I.e., independent testing suggests NooK was roughly WBridge5-strength at declarer play, not superhuman.

**NukkAI since 2022.** NukkAI (founded 2018 by Jean-Baptiste Fantun and Véronique Ventos) never published a full NooK system paper; its published bridge research is the αμ search line ([arXiv:1911.07960](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07960), [arXiv:2101.12639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12639)). The company has **pivoted away from bridge**: [nukk.ai](https://www.nukk.ai/) now markets "Agentic Crew Scheduling" SaaS for aviation/transport, with the 2022 challenge presented purely as a historical milestone; there's also a [Thales military data-fusion partnership](https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/worldwide/group/press_release/thales-and-nukkai-develop-ai-based-data-fusion-solution-military). Note: **no evidence** that NukkAI runs Argine — Argine is Funbridge's own engine (GOTO Games, merged with BBO ~2019; [funbridge.com/bridge-artificial-intelligence](https://funbridge.com/bridge-artificial-intelligence)). No NooK product or newer human-challenge results have appeared 2023–2026. Community notes from ~2023 mention defense research and that "a fully functional bot would be possible if they plugged their methods on an existing bot" — it never shipped ([computerbridge.se/nukkai](https://www.computerbridge.se/nukkai/)).

## 3. The disclosure problem

**The legal frame.** Under the Laws of Duplicate Bridge (Law 40, [WBF Laws](http://www.worldbridge.org/rules-regulations/2017-laws-of-duplicate-bridge/)), all partnership understandings — explicit *or implicit, including those arising from mutual experience* — must be fully disclosed to opponents; a special partnership understanding may not be used unless opponents can reasonably be expected to understand it or it is disclosed per regulation ([EBL misinformation guide](https://www.eurobridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/MISINFORMATION.pdf)). Mechanisms: convention cards / system cards filed in advance, alerts, and answers to opponents' questions. The [WBF Systems Policy](https://www.worldbridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WBFSystemsPolicy.pdf) classifies systems (natural → Highly Unusual Methods); [Brown Sticker conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_sticker) and [HUMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highly_unusual_method) are restricted to top-level events, require advance filing of system cards plus a *written suggested defense*, and HUMs are banned in most segments/events entirely.

**Implication for a self-play bot.** A learned bidding system is one giant implicit agreement between the two bot hands. To be legal against humans it must: (a) express each call's meaning as disclosable hand constraints ("you must describe your agreements, not your actual hand" — [BBO GIB descriptions doc](https://www.bridgebase.com/doc/gib_descriptions.php)); (b) keep those descriptions *accurate* on pain of misinformation rulings; and (c) stay within the event's system policy — a freely self-played system would likely land in Brown Sticker/HUM territory and could be simply illegal in most events, or require published defenses. Bids whose meaning the bot itself cannot state are effectively concealed partnership understandings.

**Prior art on disclosable machine bidding.**

- **GIB/BBO practice**: GIB self-alerts every bid with an explanation generated from its rule database ([BBO forum: "Full Disclosure, robot style"](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/52270-full-disclosure-robot-style/)); the chronic complaint is that GIB's descriptions are sometimes wrong (it violates its own rules), i.e., machine misinformation is a real, observed failure mode.
- **Ben/GIBBO**: BBO's neural robots dodge the problem by anchoring to human systems — Ben was trained on human BBO deals; GIBBO (live on BBO, Jan 2026) "keeps the same bidding systems while adding neural network support" ([BBO robots comparison](https://news.bridgebase.com/bridge-robots-comparison/), [Bridgerama on GIBBO](https://www.bridgerama-plus.com/en/2026/01/21/meet-gibbo/), [GIBBO convention card thread](https://www2.dal09.sl.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/91529-gibbo-convention-card-and-carding/)).
- **Academic**: Rong et al. note explicitly that bridge rules require players to explain their system and forbid hidden meanings ([arXiv:1903.00900](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.00900)); a Sensors 2022 paper built bid-meaning *visualization* for human–AI interaction ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22051877)); the self-play "specialized conventions" incompatibility problem is discussed in the ad-hoc-cooperation literature ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/10/17/3187)). NukkAI's stated founding goal included extracting human-readable rules (explainable AI), but no disclosable learned *bidding* system was ever demonstrated. **No one has yet fielded a self-play bidding system with legally adequate, verified disclosure** — practical routes: pin to a human system card (Ben/GIBBO route), constrain learning so meanings stay describable, or auto-generate the convention card + defenses from the learned policy (open research).

## 4. What top-level evaluation looks like

**Format.** The [Bermuda Bowl](https://www.worldbridge.org/competitions/wbf-championships/world-bridge-teams-championships/the-bermuda-bowl) (biennial, odd years, ~24 national teams): complete round robin of 16-board matches scored in IMPs converted to Victory Points; top 8 to knockouts; KO matches run in 16-board segments with semifinals historically 64–96 boards and **finals 96 boards** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Bowl)). Teams-of-four, two tables, same boards both rooms, net IMP difference per board.

**Variance and match length.** BBO forum analysis of >200,000 team games gives a per-board net-IMP standard deviation of ~5.4 (one analysis: match SD ≈ 7.51·√N including both-table effects) ([BBO forum: Bridge Hand Statistics](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/85159-bridge-hand-statistics-question/)). So over N boards, noise ≈ 5.4·√N IMPs: a 96-board final has ±~53 IMP one-sigma noise — a 100-board match cannot reliably separate two strong teams; the same forum analysis concludes ~500 boards begins to give confidence. General rule: to detect a true edge of δ IMPs/board at ~2σ you need **N ≈ (2·5.4/δ)² boards** — for a large 0.5 IMP/board edge that's ~470 boards; for a realistic top-vs-top edge of 0.1–0.2 IMP/board, thousands to >10,000 boards. Design implications for a credible superiority claim: long multi-session matches, duplicated boards across rooms (the teams format itself is the variance-reduction device), cross-imping over many human pairs, pre-registered statistics — plus non-deterministic, adapting human opponents (the exact thing the NooK format lacked).

## 5. Human benchmarks: how strong are today's bots?

- **BBO GIB / Ben / Big Ben vs. the BBO population** ([Robot Rankings, Nov 2024](https://news.bridgebase.com/2024/11/18/robot-rankings-november-2024/)): in robot-vs-field matchpoint comparisons across six formats (fields of 633–17,549 humans), **Advanced GIB scored 54–61% MPs** (e.g., 61.57% in Free Super Sunday, 59.22% in Premium Daylong, 60.80% in Just Declare), consistently above the human field average; **Big Ben** was close behind (e.g., 60.26%, and 55.83% in the Zenith Daylong vs. 1,783 stronger players); Basic GIB above-average; original Ben weakest in premium fields. Interpretation: clearly better than the *average* online human, which is far below expert level — these fields are mostly club-strength players, and GIB's known holes (competitive bidding especially) get exploited by experts ([GIB robot strength thread](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/73047-gib-robot-strength)). ACBL-Ben trails Advanced GIB in declarer play, matches it on defense ([BBO robots comparison](https://news.bridgebase.com/bridge-robots-comparison/)).
- **Argine (Funbridge)**: plays 5M+ deals/day, supports six human bidding systems, samples deals to interpret auctions; Funbridge publishes **no** strength-vs-human evaluation ([Who is Argine](https://funbridge.zendesk.com/hc/en-001/articles/360013508800-Who-is-Argine)).
- **Reference points at the top**: Jack lost narrowly to top Dutch pairs in full bridge (2005–06); NooK ≈ WBridge5 at declarer play per independent replay; in bidding research, learned systems are benchmarked *against WBridge5* — but WBridge5 itself is nowhere near top-human bidding, so "beats WBridge5" ≠ expert-level. Net: **current bots ≈ strong club / weak expert at full bridge; nothing demonstrated at Bermuda Bowl-pair level.**

## Synthesis: what "superhuman bridge" would actually require

1. **Full game, not fragments**: bidding (including competitive auctions), declarer play with auction inference, and *defense* — the hardest, least-solved third (partnership signaling and cooperation with an information-limited partner). Every headline result to date (Par Contest, NooK) carved out declarer play only.
2. **Legal, disclosable bidding**: either adopt a filed human system or solve explainable learned bidding — accurate machine-generated convention cards, alerts, and (if methods are unusual) written defenses, per Law 40 and the WBF Systems Policy. Undisclosable self-play conventions are illegal, and wrong explanations are misinformation.
3. **Robustness to varied, adaptive human opponents**, including deception in both directions — not exploitation of a single deterministic bot (the core NooK criticism).
4. **A credible evaluation protocol**: duplicate teams format against named top partnerships, hundreds to thousands of boards (per-board IMP SD ~5.4 ⇒ ~(11/δ)² boards to resolve a δ IMP/board edge), pre-agreed conditions, and ideally play within an established championship structure (16-board segments, 96-board-final scale, and beyond).
5. **A benchmark ecosystem**: the official World Computer-Bridge Championship has been dormant since 2019, and the one company that claimed a breakthrough (NukkAI) has exited bridge — so a serious program would need to rebuild both the bot-vs-bot baseline (WBridge5, Micro Bridge, Ben/GIBBO) and the human-challenge pipeline essentially from scratch.
