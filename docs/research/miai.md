# Miai (MIT, Zhiyuan Fan) — competitor/baseline notes

> Added 2026-08-21. Source: r/bridge post "I built Miai, a full-contract bridge bot trained [from scratch]" (Aug 2026), https://bridge.miai.moe, its changelog and system document, the public bid-query API, and the author's publications.

## What it is

- **Full-game bot (bidding + card play) trained from scratch by self-play, no human gameplay data.** Author: Zhiyuan Fan, Ph.D. student with Gabriele Farina's group at MIT ("Game Solving Lab" — equilibrium computation / extensive-form games). GitHub: [nanomena](https://github.com/nanomena) (LiteEFG contributor). Claims to be the first full-contract agent trained this way — consistent with our [literature review](literature-review.md), which found no published full-game learned agent.
- **Architecture/training (from changelog):** "v7 pair are two independently trained agents whose natural bidding stays consistent with each other; v7 is trained with a new regularization-aware self-play method." Versions: v6 (2026-06-20), v7 (2026-06-22); models `v7a`/`v7b` exposed. **"Level 0"** = raw policy net for bidding and play, *no search layer*; "Level 1" (used briefly in May) presumably adds search. The website runs Level 0 "due to computational constraints."
- Likely method lineage: Fan & Farina, *GAE Falls Short in Imperfect-Information Self-Play RL* (arXiv 2605.19235, May 2026) — proposes **VRPO** (PPO with a centralized action-value critic and Expected-SARSA(λ) advantage traces) for Dou Dizhu and HUNL poker. Not about bridge, but almost certainly the RL machinery under Miai. The "regularization-aware" self-play ties to Farina-group work on regularization in EFG solving (e.g., *The Power of Regularization in Solving EFGs*).

## Strength claims

- **+0.21 IMP/board vs WBridge5 over 1,024 duplicate boards, full game** (Level 0, the weak instant-play version). Contracts reached were worth **+0.59 IMP/board by double-dummy**, so play (Level 0, no search) gives back ~0.38 IMP/board of the bidding edge.
- "Beats the bots I've been able to test against" (unspecified). Unknown vs top humans.
- Site stats (576 single-player boards as of Aug 2026): Miai ~ +0.9 IMP/board vs its human challengers (web visitors; weak field).
- Interpretation: bidding-only DD edge of +0.59 is *below* the published SL+RL bidding SOTA vs WBridge5 (Kita +1.24, Qiu +0.98, both DD-scored) — but those systems were warm-started on WBridge5's own data and don't play cards. Miai's number is a **full-game, real-play** result, which is the metric that matters, and the first such public number for a learned agent.

## The bidding system it invented

- Mostly natural, interpretable: 1NT = balanced 13–17 HCP-ish; 1-level suit openings natural; **all two-level suit openings including 2♣ are weak/preemptive** (no strong 2♣); raises and game/slam bids are direct placements; no transfers/relays found.
- The system document is generated by **probing the policy with millions of sampled hands** per auction prefix (e.g., 4,000,000 probes for the opening seat) and reporting frequency + hand-feature bands. Features are "**Miai Points**" — calibrated honor weights **A=5, K=3, Q=1.5, J=0.5** ("Base Value") plus per-suit "Quality" (A/K=10, Q=9.5, J=9, T=8.5, small=7.5) and "Value" — i.e., the bot's learned valuation is close to modern ace-heavy/jack-light evaluation, and preempts are driven by suit Quality rather than total strength.
- Notable for our Phase 4 disclosure problem: this is a working example of **auto-generating a disclosable system card from a learned policy** by sampling — exactly the approach we sketched. Also notable: two independently trained agents converged to mutually consistent natural systems ("natural consensus"), evidence against the "self-play always invents alien conventions" worry, at least under their regularization.

## Public surface

- Website: single-player (human + Miai vs 2 Miai) and multiplayer rooms over WebSocket; no downloads, no code, no paper yet.
- **Bid-query API** (`POST /api/bidding/query`): returns the full bidding-policy distribution (temperature 1.0) for a hand/auction, plus legal actions and a seeded deal. Card format is rank+suit (`"AS"`). Models listable at `/api/bidding/models`. Usable for small-scale comparisons; hammering it for thousands of boards would be rude without asking.
- No Blue Chip table-manager interface, so it can't slot into our harness today.

## Implications for BridgeBot

1. **Someone credible is already on our lane** — Farina's group has deep equilibrium/self-play expertise and a working full-game bot. The "nobody is defending the throne" premise in [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) is now weaker: expect a paper within months (likely with a Level-1 search version and a much bigger WBridge5 margin).
2. **The bar for a SOTA claim just rose**: beating WBridge5 by a few tenths of an IMP/board is no longer novel. Our claim must either beat Miai head-to-head or clearly exceed its margin under a reproducible protocol.
3. **Validates two of our bets**: (a) full-game evaluation against WBridge5 over ~1k duplicated boards is the right currency (Miai used exactly this), and (b) policy-sampling system cards solve disclosure in practice.
4. **Opportunities**: our harness (open TM server + zero-click WBridge5 automation) is something Miai lacks publicly; offering it, or a standardized WBridge5 benchmark, is a natural collaboration hook. The author explicitly invited feedback (fanzy at mit dot edu). Also, Miai's Level-0 play leaks ~0.4 IMP/board of its bidding edge — search-based play (our Phase 2 focus) is where a complementary advantage lies.
5. **Action items**: (a) reach out to the author (user decision); (b) add Miai as a named baseline/competitor in PLAN Phase 3; (c) watch arXiv for the Miai paper; (d) consider a small, polite bid-agreement comparison between our future bidder and the v7 API.
